# Stage 4 media and publication durability hardening

Major Stage 4 now carries checksum-bound private media through R2 verification and keeps publication execution durable across dispatch, workers, retries, reconciliation, and republish. PostgreSQL remains authoritative for tenant boundaries, transitions, leases, target history, reconciliation claims, denial audits, and archive invalidation. All sixteen original findings and all five follow-up findings are resolved in the current tracked and untracked change set. **Watch for:** no blocking behavioral correctness issue remains in the reviewed Stage 4 diff.

**Verdict**: APPROVED

## High-level view

The media path uses one base64 SHA-256 value from browser hashing through signed R2 PUT headers, provider HEAD metadata, and completion. Public reads remain exact-key authorizations; mandatory credential-free contracts cover signature binding, object lifecycle behavior, and private anonymous denial while optional managed-provider checks remain available.

Publication targets own immutable terminal history, while Article Site is explicitly the mutable current projection. PostgreSQL guards the exact five-state transition relation and the deliberate terminal-to-queued republish edge; dispatch exhaustion produces terminal targets and a readable failed result.

Database-time lease checks revoke expired workers on target fetch, transition, and release. Recovery respects `maxAttempts`, one target budget spans the invocation, release derives its next due time from incomplete targets, and reconciliation uses exclusive claims or claim-token/expiry compare-and-set updates.

Public authorization requires an active Article for Article-owned media. Archive atomically unions active published Article references by owner or `lead_media_id` with Site Settings references, deduplicates by Site, and emits invalidation work; unavailable rows retain the non-disclosing denial shape and denial audits contain no foreign identifier.

## Checksum-bound exact-object media contract

`ObjectStoragePort.authorizeExactPut` carries the browser's SHA-256 digest, R2 signs `x-amz-checksum-sha256` as an unhoistable header, and completion accepts provider SHA-256 metadata rather than an ETag (`app/cms/cms-workspace.tsx`, `src/ports/object-storage.ts`, `src/infrastructure/storage/r2-object-storage.ts`, `src/application/stage4/media-service.ts`). The provider suite combines production AWS signing with a credential-free contract for checksum validation, PUT/HEAD/GET/delete, exact-key scope, and unsigned-read denial (`tests/integration/stage4-provider-live.integration.test.ts`). This resolves original findings 1 and 15.

## Immutable history and guarded Article Site projection

`publishing_job_targets` persists terminal URL and timestamp, and terminal trigger behavior preserves outcome fields on repeated writes. Republish creates a new queued target before Article Site can move from `published` or `failed` to `queued`; all other Job, target, and Article Site transitions remain within the approved relation (`drizzle/0006_stage4_media_publication_runtime.sql`, `src/infrastructure/db/schema/operations.ts`). Live PostgreSQL coverage proves prior-job URL stability, rejects invalid rewinds, and projects dispatch exhaustion as a zero-success failed result. This resolves findings 2–4.

## Database-time fencing and bounded worker progress

Production target discovery, transition, and release require the current owner/fence plus `lease_expires_at > clock_timestamp()`. Expired recovery fails targets at `maxAttempts`; otherwise it returns only incomplete work to retry. `PublicationWorker.run` decrements one invocation-wide target budget, while release schedules queued work immediately and preserves the earliest retry target's future due time when no queued work remains (`src/application/stage4/publication-worker.ts`, `src/infrastructure/db/repositories/drizzle-stage4-repository.ts`). This resolves findings 5–7 and the follow-up retry-schedule issue.

## Receipt, dispatch, and cleanup reconciliation

A target transition, its audit, and its unacknowledged receipt commit atomically. Receipt existence is therefore commit evidence: reconciliation changes only acknowledgement/claim fields under a live claim-token/expiry predicate, independent of later cyclic target progress. Dispatch and cleanup discovery claim bounded rows with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`; cleanup becomes terminal after retry exhaustion. In-memory and live PostgreSQL cases cover cyclic receipt progress, overlapping receipt/cleanup claims, release scheduling, and terminal cleanup (`drizzle/0006_stage4_media_publication_runtime.sql`, `src/infrastructure/db/repositories/drizzle-stage4-repository.ts`). This resolves findings 8–10 and the follow-up receipt and production-coverage issues.

## Public revocation, archive invalidation, and denial boundaries

Only `articles.status = 'active'` maps to a public-eligible Article. Archive locks the media row and, in the same transaction, selects Site Settings plus active published Article Sites reached through media ownership or `articles.lead_media_id`, deduplicates Site IDs, inserts invalidation tasks, and appends the audit (`src/infrastructure/db/repositories/drizzle-stage4-repository.ts`). Missing, foreign, or inactive media remains unavailable; 409 is reserved for a verified stale version on a same-tenant active row. Denial audits use the requesting Organization, a null target ID, and sanitized reason (`src/application/stage4/media-service.ts`, `src/application/stage4/publication-service.ts`). This resolves findings 11–14 and the follow-up draft/reference issues.
